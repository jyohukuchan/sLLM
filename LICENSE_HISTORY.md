## Pre-reset history

Historical versions in the ancestry of the former `main` branch carried the
Apache License 2.0 as their top-level project license. The repository reset and
the MIT licensing of the current snapshot do not purport to withdraw, revoke, or
retroactively alter permissions that each applicable rights holder validly
granted for those historical versions. File-specific and third-party terms may
also have applied.

The reset preserved the pre-reset commits as ancestors of the current `main`
branch by explicit project decision. In this document, "historical" or
"archived" describes their role; it does not mean those commits are unreachable
from current Git refs. Separate verified bundles and archive refs also preserve
the reset state. Rewriting shared history or force-pushing solely to make the old
commits unreachable is not part of the current plan.

The archived history is therefore not evidence that every old file may be copied
into the current MIT snapshot. Before reintroducing code or other material from a
pre-reset commit, determine for that material:

- its author and copyright holder;
- the license that applied to the exact historical version;
- whether it contains or derives from third-party material;
- which notice, attribution, source-offer, or other license conditions apply; and
- whether it may be combined and redistributed in the intended form.

Record any approved third-party import according to
[`docs/provenance/README.md`](docs/provenance/README.md). If ownership or license
provenance cannot be established, do not reintroduce the material until it has
been reviewed by the rights holder or appropriate counsel.
